## The shared thesis

From `00`, and it applies to everything here:

> **Loved: systems that are legible and alive.
> Disliked: systems that are opaque and demanding.**
>
> Every system must be readable at a glance and must run without you. The player's
> job is to make decisions, never to perform maintenance.

The test for any proposed mechanic: *if the player ignores this for ten minutes, is
the result "something interesting happened" or "a chore piled up"?*

And from `03`, the rule that governs granularity everywhere:

> **The player's brush is the smallest thing that changes the simulation.
> Everything below that resolution is generated by the world, not placed by you.**
>
> A mechanic must be **spatial** (*where*, not *how much* or *how often*) and
> **consequential** (two different placements must produce different outcomes).
> You draw; you never dispatch.

## Shared machinery

Both games use: visible agents with legible desires; player-authored placement with
buildings that age and evolve afterwards; real-time with pause; the no-numbers,
read-it-by-looking UI experiment; named characters accumulating history; and a
small, dense, toy scale.

The territory/pressure system in `01` is specific to Game B.

## Order

**Game A first.** Bounded scope, it de-risks every shared system cheaply, the
aesthetic is far more distinctive, and it has a natural ending. Game B inherits a
proven engine afterwards. Reasoning in `02` §1.
